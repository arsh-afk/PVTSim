CMAT-00184 · AQUASOLAR LIVING FINAL REPORT

Why this simulation was needed

A villa combines changing weather, household hot-water demand, electricity use, and water-treatment loads. A transient simulation lets the team compare those coupled needs across seasons and test design choices before relying on a field trial. The report's motivation includes household bottled-water use despite treated municipal supply, reject water from conventional point-of-use RO, and PV and solar-thermal technologies competing for roof space.

Report results are not live backend outputs. The submitted report describes a full-system Python screening model, TRNSYS 17 cross-validation in progress, and a test rig under development. The current web runner executes the PVT collector only; tank, RO, electrical-bus dispatch, and economics are not implemented here.

01

SOLAR INPUT → CO-GENERATION

PVT collector · heat and electricity

Report equations (1)–(2)
InputsPlane-of-array irradiance G · collector area A · ambient/inlet temperatures · fluid flow ṁ · collector coefficients
  1. 1

    Estimate useful heat and fluid outlet temperature

    Report Eq. (1)
    Q˙u=A[η0G−a1(Tin−Ta)]1+Aa12m˙cp Tout=Tin+Q˙um˙cp

    Optical solar gain is reduced by the temperature-driven collector loss; mass flow determines the useful outlet-temperature rise.

  2. 2

    Convert irradiance to cell electrical output

    Report Eq. (2)
    Pel=AGηref[1−β(Tc−25)]fd Tc={Tfm+kcGpump onTa+knfGpump off

    Electrical conversion falls as cell temperature rises; the report uses distinct cell-temperature estimates for flowing and stagnant conditions.

OutputsUseful heat Q̇u · outlet temperature Tout · electric power Pel · cell temperature Tc
02

COLLECTOR HEAT → HOT-WATER SERVICE

Stratified tank and delivery mixing

Report equations (3)–(4)
InputsCollector/auxiliary heat · node flow transfers · tank ambient losses · demand and delivery temperatures
  1. 3

    Update energy stored in each tank node

    Report Eq. (3)
    MicpdTidt=∑j→im˙j→icp(Tj−Ti)+UAi(Ta−Ti)+Q˙aux,i

    Each of six nodes gains or loses energy through inter-node flow, tank losses to ambient, and auxiliary heating.

  2. 4

    Mix hot tank water to the delivery setpoint

    Report Eq. (4)
    m˙hot=m˙dem(Tdel−Tmains)(Ttop−Tmains)

    The tank-top and mains-water temperatures set how much hot tank water is needed to meet the requested delivery temperature.

OutputsTank-node temperature history Ti · delivered hot-water flow ṁhot
03

MUNICIPAL WATER → PRODUCT + REUSE

Reverse osmosis and zero-reject water balance

Report equations (5)–(6)
InputsFeed flow and salinity · pressure · membrane permeance · temperature and fouling corrections · recovery R
  1. 5

    Estimate membrane permeate flow

    Report Eq. (5)
    Qp=KwTCFFF(ΔP−πfb−πp) TCF=e2640(1298.15−1273.15+T),π=0.7584X

    Net driving pressure, membrane condition, and temperature determine product-water flow.

  2. 6

    Close the concentrate, salinity, and pumping balances

    Report Eq. (6)
    Qb=Qp1−RR,Xb=Xf−RXp1−R Pbp=QfΔPηbp,SEC=Pbp+PauxQp

    Recovery partitions product and concentrate flow; a separate salinity balance tracks brine concentration, while pump power determines specific energy.

OutputsProduct Qp · reused brine Qb, Xb · pump power Pbp · SEC
04

ENERGY QUANTITY → ENERGY QUALITY

Solar exergy and system efficiencies

Report equations (7)–(8)
InputsSolar heat input and sun/dead-state temperatures · electricity · useful hot water · component fuel and product exergy
  1. 7

    Convert solar heat to exergy; score each component

    Report Eq. (7)
    Exsun=Qsun[1−43T0Ts+13(T0Ts)4] ηII,k=ExP,kExF,k

    The Petela factor estimates the maximum work available from solar radiation; component efficiency compares useful product exergy with supplied fuel exergy.

  2. 8

    Aggregate first- and second-law system efficiency

    Report Eq. (8)
    ηI=Wvilla+QDHW+Wmin,ROQsun ηII=Wvilla+ExDHW+Wmin,ROExsun

    Energy-based efficiency and exergy-based efficiency answer different questions; low-temperature heat has less work potential than electricity.

OutputsSolar exergy Exsun · component and system ηI, ηII
05

SIMULATED PERFORMANCE → FINANCIAL METRICS

Capital recovery, lifecycle cost, and payback

Report equations (9)–(10)
InputsInstalled capital · annual costs and replacements · discount rate · project life · annual savings · drinking-water production
  1. 9

    Annualize capital and discount lifecycle costs

    Report Eq. (9)
    CRF=i(1+i)n(1+i)n−1 LCC=CAPEX+∑y=1nCy+Ry(1+i)y

    The capital recovery factor converts investment to an equivalent annual amount; lifecycle cost adds discounted operating and replacement costs.

  2. 10

    Compute simple payback and levelized water cost

    Report Eq. (10)
    SPB=ΔCAPEXΔC LCOW=CAPEXWCRF+CO&MW+Cwater+Cel,ROVdrink

    Payback compares extra installed cost with annual savings; LCOW spreads annualized water-system cost over drinking-water volume.

OutputsCapital recovery CRF · lifecycle cost LCC · simple payback SPB · levelized water cost LCOW
